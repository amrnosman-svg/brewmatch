What your coffee habit really costs per year

We worked out what nine common coffee setups cost per year, assuming two drinks a day. For most of them the machine is the smaller part of the bill. What you put in it costs more.

Quick takeaways
  • If you use pods, the pods are almost the whole cost: 90% to 95% of the yearly total in the pod setups below.
  • The same super-automatic costs about $425 a year with grocery-store coffee and about $1,105 with specialty beans. That is 2.6 times as much for the same machine.
  • A Nespresso Original pod (about $0.90) costs a little less than the beans in a specialty double shot ($0.94 to $1.30).
  • Original machines with compatible pods ($405 a year), a super-automatic with grocery beans ($425) and K-Cups ($440) all cost about the same.

When a more expensive machine pays for itself

Say you drink two Vertuo pods a day ($920 a year) and switch to a $700-class super-automatic running on grocery-store beans. You would spend about $495 less per year, and the extra $550 up front pays for itself in about 13 months.

That only holds if you keep buying ordinary beans. Switch to specialty beans and the Vertuo setup ($920) costs less than the super-automatic ($1,105). What you put in the machine matters more than which machine you buy.

What these numbers leave out

  • Milk, syrups and sugar.
  • Paper filters for drip and pour-over, and a grinder or kettle if you start with whole beans.
  • Electricity, shipping, tax and sales.
  • Machines that die early, or last longer than we assumed.

These are estimates, not quotes. Coffee got a lot more expensive in 2025 and 2026, so plug in what you actually pay.
